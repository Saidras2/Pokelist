# Route A5 and Route A8 — flush magnetic lid, self-assembly, no glue

Two versions of the same case. Everything is identical except the **wall
thickness**, and that one number decides the magnet type, the size, the cost
and how well it survives a drop.

## The one number that decides everything

A flush lid means the lid is a plain plate resting on the walls' rim, so the
magnets have to sit **vertically in the rim** — and the rim is only as wide as
the wall is thick.

| Wall | Magnet | Acrylic each side | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 mm | 4 mm | 0.5 mm | **cracks** |
| 5 mm | 3 mm | 1.0 mm | risky |
| 5 mm | 3 × 8 mm slot flush to the inner face | 2.0 mm outboard | **A5 — works** |
| 6 mm | 3 mm | 1.5 mm | acceptable |
| 8 mm | 4 mm | 2.0 mm | **A8 — comfortable** |

A5 gets away with 5 mm by cutting the pocket as a **slot that is flush with the
wall's inner face**, so the acrylic is all on the outside where it matters. The
trade is that the slot is open on the inside — hidden by the booster box, but
it is a notch in the inner top edge.

A8 just uses ordinary round pockets with 2 mm of acrylic all round, and gets
thicker walls into the bargain.

## Making it survive a drop

You asked for compact, connected and strong. Here is what actually does the
work, in order of how much it matters.

### 1. The foam liner — the single biggest factor

The foam is what saves the booster box. It decouples the box from the case, so
on impact the box decelerates over 5 mm of crush instead of instantly. That
alone cuts the peak force on the box by roughly **5–10×**.

It also stops the box rattling, which is what causes the corner scuffing that
makes a sealed box lose value.

**Do not skip the foam to save 30 cents.** Everything else on this list is
about the case surviving; the foam is about the *box* surviving.

### 2. Wall thickness — this is why A8 exists

Impact resistance scales roughly with the **cube** of thickness for deflection
and the square for stiffness. Going from 5 mm to 8 mm is a 60% increase in
thickness and about a **2.5× increase in the energy the case can take before
it cracks**. That is the whole argument for A8.

If the case is going to be posted, or handled by anyone other than you, take A8.

### 3. Radius every internal corner — R2 minimum

A sharp internal corner in acrylic has a theoretically **infinite** stress
concentration. It is where every crack starts. A laser can cut any radius you
like, so there is no excuse for a square internal corner anywhere:

- the root of every finger on the corner joints
- the rim of every magnet pocket
- the corners of the base plate's notches
- the ends of the finger-lift scallop

### 4. Anneal after cutting — the cheapest upgrade there is

Laser cutting leaves locked-in stress at the cut edge, and that stress is
exactly why laser-cut acrylic shatters instead of flexing. Two to three hours
at **80 °C**, then cool slowly **inside the oven** with the door shut.

This is a genuine, large improvement in impact resistance and almost nobody
does it.

### 5. Polish the cut edges

A laser-cut edge is a row of micro-cracks. A quick flame polish (small torch,
fast sweeping passes, ~150 mm away) or a wet sand to 1500 grit blunts them.
Micro-cracks are crack starters; remove them and the case gets noticeably
tougher.

### 6. Box-jointed corners spread the load

A butt joint fails along one line. A finger joint spreads the same impact
across nine interlocking fingers per corner, and each finger is trapped by its
neighbours. That is why the case is joined this way rather than glued — the
glue would be the weak link, and it would fail before the acrylic did.

### What will still happen — the honest part

**The magnetic lid will pop off in a hard drop.** Run the numbers: the lid
plate on A8 weighs 168 g, and a 1 m drop onto concrete decelerates at roughly
500 g, so the lid sees about **84 kg of inertial force**. Four magnet pairs hold
about 1.4 kg. No practical magnet array holds that.

This is why the foam matters so much. When the lid comes off, the foam cradle
still holds the box. The case loses a lid; you do not lose the contents.

If you genuinely need the lid to stay on through a drop, it has to be
**mechanically** retained — a tongue on the lid that engages a lip inside the
walls. But then the case takes the entire impact itself and cracks instead.
There is a real trade here: a lid that pops off is a lid that releases energy.

**Test it rather than trusting the numbers.** Drop a prototype from 1 m onto
concrete in all six orientations. What you are looking for is: foam intact, box
undamaged, case cracked at most at a seam and not shattered into pieces.

## Assembly — eight steps

Illustrations: `variants/assembly-01.svg` … `variants/assembly-08.svg`, or see
them all in a row in [`compare-a.html`](compare-a.html).

| # | Step | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Lay out every part.** 9 acrylic panels, 16 magnets, 6 foam pads. Nothing is glued in the factory. | Check the panels against the cut list first |
| 2 | **Drill the 16 magnet pockets** — every wall gets 2, the lid and base plates get 4 each. | Do it while the panels are flat. Brad-point bit, ~500 rpm, backer board, tape the depth |
| 3 | **Bond the 8 wall magnets** — 4 in the top edges, 4 in the bottom edges. | Set them flush. Proud magnets stop the lid seating |
| 4 | **Bond the lid and base magnets** — 8 more, at the matching positions. | Offer them dry and let the wall magnets pull each one round the right way before you glue. That solves polarity |
| 5 | **Build the two halves.** Left + front wall (front slides in −X), right + back wall (back slides in +X). | A press fit goes by hand. If you need a mallet, the kerf is wrong |
| 6 | **Slide the halves together in −X.** Both remaining corners mesh at once. | Check both diagonals are equal |
| 7 | **Drop the tube onto the base**, then lay in the foam. | The base locates on four notches, then the magnets clamp it |
| 8 | **Box in, lid on.** | To open: press one corner and the far corner tips up, or hook a nail into the front rim scallop |

### Why the finger-lift scallop is there

A perfectly flush lid has **nothing to grip**. The 20 × 4 mm scallop in the
front wall's top rim is the one place the rectangle is not perfect — and it is
the difference between a case that opens easily and one that generates support
emails.

## Which one

- **A8** if the case is going to be shipped, or handled by anyone but you, or
  you simply want the strongest object. It costs about $2.50 more and is 6 mm
  bigger in each direction. It is the one I would put in a box and post.
- **A5** if you want the thinner, glassier look and the case will live on a
  shelf. It still works — the slot pockets are genuinely fine — but you are
  relying on a 2 mm wall, and 5 mm acrylic takes about 40% of the impact that
  8 mm does.
